Vladimir
Filkin

More than eight years of research in computational mechanics, electromagnetics and coupled multiphysics problems.

Doctoral researcher at the Institute of Mechanics and Mechatronics, TU Wien.

Current research

Induction heating of thin steel sheets and belts is a tightly coupled problem: the sheet heats up, expands and buckles, and the buckled shape changes the electromagnetic field and, with it, the temperature distribution.

We tackle it from all three sides: analytical models that explain the post-buckling behaviour, fully coupled finite element simulations that combine a structural shell model with eddy-current and heat-conduction models, and a full-scale experimental campaign that validates both.

This is applied research within the FFG BRIDGE project Induction-Buckling, together with Berndorf Band GmbH.
